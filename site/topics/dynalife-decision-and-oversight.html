Direction, business case, and board delegation

Primary records distinguish ministerial strategic direction, the auditor's finding on the absence of a formal business case, and the AHS board's January 27, 2022 term-sheet approval and conditional CEO delegation.

Ministerial and department strategic direction (OAG finding)

Evidence status: documented.

The Auditor General found that the then health minister and Department of Health expected the 2019–22 community laboratory outsourcing to proceed, while AHS management and board were not the strategic decision-makers. This finding does not attribute initiation to Danielle Smith.

Absence of a documented formal business case (OAG finding)

Evidence status: documented.

The Auditor General found no evidence that AHS or Alberta Health developed a formal business case before initiating the procurement. This is an audit finding subject to the auditor’s stated indeterminate access limitation, not proof that no other analysis existed.

AHS board term-sheet approval and conditional CEO delegation

Evidence status: documented.

AHS’s board unanimously approved AHS and APL entering a DynaLIFE term sheet on January 27, 2022 and conditionally delegated CEO authority to execute a later services agreement if its final fundamental terms were not substantively different. The Auditor General separately reports board approval of the contract based on value. The final condition and signatories remain unverified.

Dispute over records access during the Auditor General examination

Evidence status: disputed. Disputed; accounts differ.

The Auditor General said AHS restricted access to records with Department of Health support and that the impact of missing or redacted material was indeterminate. Premier Smith told the Assembly the government fully cooperated and disputed suggestions otherwise. The available records do not establish personal intent behind the access limits.

Written RFP met standards; Sonic withdrew citing pandemic travel limits

Evidence status: documented.

The Auditor General found the written RFP met applicable document standards. After Sonic Healthcare withdrew citing pandemic travel restrictions, an independent procurement adviser said AHS could negotiate with the remaining qualified proponent. This did not resolve the auditor’s separate concerns about proceeding with one bidder.

AHS and Premier Smith rationale for outsourcing and reversal

Evidence status: attributed claim. Attributed claim.

AHS described improving service delivery as the purpose of bringing DynaLIFE operations under APL in 2023. The Auditor General reports that savings were the primary earlier objective of outsourcing. Premier Smith later said her government reversed course after seeing DynaLIFE could not manage provincewide service.
