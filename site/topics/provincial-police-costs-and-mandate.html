Budgets, Forecasts, and Municipal Cost-Sharing (Separate Bases)

Keeps 2024-27 voted Government Estimates, 2021 PwC forecasts, federal RCMP cost-share rates, and municipal PFM contribution percentages strictly separate.

Voted Alberta Sheriffs operating budgets rose from $136.7M (2024-25) and $156.0M (original 2025-26) to $200.942M in 2026-27

Evidence status: documented.

Reconciling Alberta's 2025-26 and 2026-27 Government Estimates on a single voted-budget basis, Program 2.1 (renamed Alberta Sheriffs Police Service Funding) rose from $136.738M (2024-25 Budget) and $156.035M (original 2025-26 Estimate; restated to $164.066M Comparable Budget after an intra-ministry +$8.031M reclassification from Law Enforcement Oversight alongside splitting out the Police Review Commission) to $200.942M in 2026-27, incorporating +$36.9M for ASPS implementation. Program 2.4 Contract Policing and Policing Oversight rose from $397.192M to $432.289M, bringing the combined two-program Estimates total from $553.227M (original 2025-26; $561.258M restated) to $633.231M. Separate 2026-27 PSES Business Plan allocations include $14.9M for border Sheriffs Interdiction Patrol Teams and $26.4M for the Police Review Commission.

Separate bases: 2021 PwC forecast (~$366M transition, $735M-$759M/yr) vs. 2026-2031 municipal cost-share ramp (22% to 30%)

Evidence status: documented.

Two distinct non-summable financial baselines frame Alberta's policing debate. Under Premier Jason Kenney, the October 2021 PwC Transition Study forecast roughly $366M over six years in transition costs (about $241M operating and $125M capital) and $735M to $759M in annual operating costs for a full provincial police force, alongside roughly $170M per year in federal RCMP cost-sharing (30% under PPSA; 30% or 10% under MPSAs). Separately, under Premier Danielle Smith, the Renewed Police Funding Model effective April 1, 2026 increases rural and small-municipality shares of actual frontline PPSA costs from 22% in 2026-27 by two percentage points annually to 30% in 2030-31+, while adding a Vacancy Modifier and Population Density Subsidy.

Public Mandate & Official Government Rationale

Documents the December 2025 Alberta Next Panel polling and recommendation, the ten October 19, 2026 referendum questions, and Hansard explanations from Ministers Amery and Ellis.

Alberta Next Panel advised against a policing referendum despite 52% polling opposition, and the October 19, 2026 ballot omitted RCMP replacement

Evidence status: documented.

In December 2025, the government-appointed Alberta Next Panel recommended transitioning community policing from the RCMP to an Alberta Police Service prior to the 2032 contract expiry without holding a referendum—arguing that over 80% of Albertans already receive policing from municipal services or Alberta Sheriffs—even though its own report recorded 52% opposition in professional polling (44%, 41%, and 48% support across three 2025 waves, lowest of seven measures) and majority opposition in online surveys. Cabinet's Orders in Council 109/2026, 110/2026, and 160/2026 set ten questions for the October 19, 2026 stand-alone referendum with zero questions on RCMP replacement.

Ministers and the Alberta Next Panel cite timely family answers, rising RCMP costs, ~500 RCMP vacancies, and the 2032 federal contract expiry

Evidence status: attributed claim. Attributed claim.

In Alberta Hansard on November 23, 2026, Justice Minister Mickey Amery stated that Bill 44 safeguards voter data, modernizes law-enforcement legislation, improves SafeRoads Alberta, and helps families obtain prompt, accurate answers after sudden deaths. On November 25, 2026, Public Safety Minister Mike Ellis told the Assembly that rising municipal policing costs flow directly from RCMP cost increases and that ASPS augments and supports existing forces. Separately, the December 2025 Alberta Next Panel report pointed to roughly 500 unfilled positions among 3,151 contracted Alberta RCMP positions and the federal government's March 10, 2025 RCMP modernization white paper ahead of the 2032 PPSA expiry.
