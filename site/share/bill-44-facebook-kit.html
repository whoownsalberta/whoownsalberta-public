2. Main Facebook caption

On November 23, 2026, Alberta tabled Bill 44—a 163-page First Reading omnibus bill (168-page PDF) amending 20 provincial laws at once.

Read the actual statutory text alongside Alberta's 2026–27 Government Estimates, Order in Council 116/2026, and the Alberta Next Panel report, and four structural shifts stand out:

• Crown-corp firewall dissolved: Less than two years after legislating the Alberta Sheriffs Police Service as an arm's-length Crown corporation (Bill 49, 2025) and seven months after dropping the rule against board overlap (OIC 116/2026), Bill 44 repeals corporate status altogether and moves officers onto direct government payroll.
• Mandatory custodial fatality inquiries repealed: Existing Alberta law—paralleling BC, Manitoba, and Ontario statutes (with BC even requiring published reasons when waived)—requires a public fatality inquiry for deaths in police or prison custody unless purely natural. Bill 44 repeals s. 33(3), shifts screening to unpublished Cabinet regulations, makes family report access discretionary under regulation, and includes zero transitional savings clause in Section 6 for pending cases.
• Rising budgets, rural cost-share ramp, zero ballot question: Voted Sheriffs Police operating funding climbed to $200.942M in 2026–27 (+$36.9M; $633.231M combined with Contract Policing), while rural municipal policing shares ramp from 22% to 30% by 2030–31—with no updated post-2021 transition cost study (PwC's 2021 Kenney-era study forecast ~$366M transition and $735M–$759M/yr operating on a separate basis). And despite the Alberta Next Panel recording 52% polling opposition to replacing the RCMP, policing was left off all 10 October 2026 referendum questions.
• Health records linked without patient consent: Section 10 lets health custodians disclose identifiable health records without individual consent for minister-approved public-sector analytics, empowers binding disclosure orders, and lets Cabinet expand Health Information Act overrides by regulation.

(Fair context from the primary record: Bill 44 leaves Police Act s. 33.94(2) intact—barring the Minister from directing specific investigations, operations, or discipline—retains the civilian Oversight Board and Police Review Commission, codifies Crown vicarious liability, keeps OIPC privacy-assessment filings, and aligns with non-corporate ministry housing used in Ontario and Quebec. In Hansard, Ministers Amery and Ellis said Bill 44 modernizes law enforcement, gives grieving families faster answers, and augments existing police forces ahead of the 2032 federal RCMP contract expiry.)
